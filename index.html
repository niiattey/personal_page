About

Air pollution, child health & spatial data science in Sub-Saharan Africa.

I am an MPhil Epidemiology student in the Biomedical Sciences Department at the University of Cape Coast, working under the DICE Project within the NIH DS-I Africa consortium led by Prof. Kofi Amegah. My work sits at the intersection of environmental epidemiology, spatial statistics, and child health in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Within the DICE Project, I contribute to research examining the spatial variability in the health impacts of ambient PM2.5 exposure across SSA, including the modifying roles of neighbourhood greenness and nutrition. I am also involved in developing multi-temporal exposure models by integrating land use regression approaches with ground and mobile monitoring data.

Methodologically, I apply machine learning-based Land Use Regression models and Bayesian spatial techniques to investigate how air pollution influences child undernutrition and respiratory outcomes. On the applied side, I lead data management and support sensor network operations for the GhanaAQ and Breathe Accra projects, which monitor urban air quality across 13 districts in Greater Accra.

Methodology

Research & Skills

Air quality monitoring & source apportionment

Developing high-resolution exposure models using low-cost sensors and ML algorithms (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) across data-sparse African environments. Applies receptor-based source apportionment to characterise pollution sources for policy.

Spatial epidemiology & child health

Applying Bayesian hierarchical spatial models (BYM/BYM2 via R-INLA) to map disease risk across SSA. Works extensively with DHS data to quantify PM2.5 links to childhood stunting, wasting, anemia, and respiratory outcomes.

Statistical programming & GIS

Primarily R (INLA, spdep, tidyverse, sf, Shiny) and Python for ML pipelines and API integrations. Integrates satellite data (ACAG PM2.5, ERA5) with ground sensors via GIS for large-scale exposure assessment.

R Shiny · GhanaAQ Network

Clarity Node-S QC Dashboard

Launch live dashboard →

The problem

Managing high-frequency sensor data across 13 districts required manual, error-prone data cleaning and device health tracking.

The solution

An interactive dashboard that fetches data via the Clarity API, applies custom QC algorithms, and maps device health spatially.

The outcome

Automated maintenance reporting, cutting QA/QC time by over 60% and ensuring data integrity for researchers.
